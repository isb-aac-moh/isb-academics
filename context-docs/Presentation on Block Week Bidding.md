Common Terminology

• Closed Bidding: Students submit sealed bids for courses without seeing others' bids.

• Course Selection: Students can select from a list of elective courses with detailed outlines, faculty profiles, and

pre/co-requisites provided before bidding begins.

• Bid: Each student receives 4,500 bid points in total.

• Minimum & Maximum Bid Value:
• Minimum: 100 points
• Maximum: Over and above the min points (100) required per course to fulfill graduation requirement (18

elective credits).

• Second-price sealed-bid auction: Students submit closed bids; highest bidder wins but pays the lowest successful

bid price.

• Faculty-Course Combination: Each offering is tagged to a specific faculty and course for bidding purposes.

• Clearing Price: The lowest successful bid that fills the class size.

• Tiebreaker Score: Random number used to resolve ties in bids, pre-assigned and shared before bidding.

No course can be added / dropped after final phase of bidding.

General Bidding Instructions for Block Weeks
• Phases: Bidding process includes 4 structured phases from initial bidding to final add phase and counselling

round.

• The bidding rounds for Block Weeks 1 & 2 and Block Weeks 3 & 4 will be conducted simultaneously.

• Phase 1 – Add Phase (Initial sealed bidding): Students submit sealed bids for courses without seeing

others' bids.

• Phase 2 – Drop Phase: Indicative clearing prices shown; students can drop courses and get points refunded

post Phase-2.

• Waitlist: Maintained by bid rank and tiebreaker. Waitlist is dissolved post Phase-2 processing.

• Phase 4 – Add, Final Round: Round is for 3 hours (only for Block weeks) and final chance to bid for

available seats.

• Pass/Fail Option: Some courses can be converted post-bid; subject to seat caps and eligibility.

• Phase 6 – Counselling round, offline: Offline allotment of courses to students with fewer than the

minimum required term/block week courses or to meet graduation requirements.

• No Sampling Sessions or Phases 3 and 5 for Block Weeks.

Note: Only students who participate in Phase-1 will be eligible to take part in the subsequent rounds of
Block Week bidding.

Phase 1 – Add Phase (Initial sealed bidding)

• Student Actions

• Submit Sealed Bids: For faculty-course combinations, without visibility into other bids.
• Each student can bid for one course, up to the permitted maximum credit.
• Bid Points System: Total: 4,500 points per student.

• Minimum: 100 points per course.
• System reserves 100 points per remaining required credit, ensuring students have enough bid

points to complete 18 credits

• Course Materials Provided: Course outlines, faculty videos, and profiles available from first Monday of the

prior term. Students are expected to review before bidding.

• Tiebreaker:  Each student is assigned a random tiebreaker score per term (shared during Phase 1). In case of

•

tied bids, lower scores receive higher priority.
Intercampus Exchange: Students on Intercampus Exchange will receive 250 bid points for each Block Week.
For every course, 2 seats will be reserved for Intercampus students in Phase 1. In the Phase 4 Add Round,
they will bid for courses along with the base campus students.

• No opportunity to Bid in Phase-4 if student do not participate during Phase 1.

Understanding the Waitlist Mechanism
• Overview: A waitlist is created when multiple students bid for a course with limited seats.
•

If a tentatively registered student drops the course during Phase 2, the next eligible student on the waitlist is promoted post
processing.
• Ranking Criteria

• Bid Points – Higher bids are ranked first.
• Tiebreaker Score – Used when bid points are equal (lower score gets higher priority).

• How It Works: Students are ranked by:

• Bid Points (Descending)
• Tiebreaker Score (Ascending)
• Top-ranked students are tentatively registered.
• Remaining students are placed on the waitlist in order of rank.

• Post-Drop Activation

•

If a registered student drops the course in Phase 2, → the next highest-ranked waitlisted student will be tentatively
registered to the course post processing.

• The students after dropping the course (tentatively registered/ waitlist) can re-bid in Phase 4.
• The students who did not participate in Phase 1 cannot participate in Phase 4 either.
• Waitlist will be dissolved after Phase-2 processing.
Example Scenario for waitlist:
• Course 1 has 3 seats.
• Students A to I bid:

• A, B, C are tentatively registered.
• D to I are waitlisted based on rank.

Pass/Fail Conversion Round

• Eligibility & Restrictions

• Students may convert eligible courses to Pass/Fail.
• Mandatory electives cannot be taken as Pass/Fail.
• Once designated as Pass/Fail, a course cannot be reverted to credit status, and vice versa.

• Impact on Academic Record
• Pass/Fail courses:

• Do not count toward GPA/CGPA.
• Do not fulfill graduation or concentration requirements i.e. do not contribute to the minimum 18

elective credits required for graduation.

• Will appear on the transcript but are excluded from GPA/CGPA calculations.

• Grading Criteria

• To earn a ‘Pass’, students must achieve a grade equivalent to C or higher.

• Allocation Process: Course allocation is based on:

• Bid points
• Tie-breaker rules, if demand exceeds available seats.

Pass/Fail Conversion Phase – Key Points (Final Screen)

• Convert to P/F: Visible for eligible course.
• Greyed Out Icon: Indicates that conversion is not allowed due to credit constraints.

• When this option is useful:

Phase 6: Counselling Round

Purpose: Designed for students who:

• Have fewer than the minimum required term/block week courses.
• Need to fulfill graduation requirements.
• ASA assists students in selecting courses wherever seats are available.

• Process Overview

• Conducted offline by the ASA team. An offline form will be provided to students to indicate their course

preferences based on seat availability.

• Clearing Price 2 (from Phase 4) is applied to all registered courses.
• Courses allocated in this phase cannot be dropped.
• The counselling round in Block Weeks 1 and 2 will be open for students who participated in phase 1 and could

not get seat in any course at the end of the Phase 4.

• This round ensures that students meet their academic requirements and helps with course selection in cases where

they missed earlier phases (during Term 4-7) or need additional credits.
